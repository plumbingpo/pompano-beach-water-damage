Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Pompano Beach

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Pompano Beach, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Rooms Next Door

A washer hose can release water for hours. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
