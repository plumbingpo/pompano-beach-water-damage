AC Leak Water Damage in Pompano Beach: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
