Bathroom Water Damage: What Pompano Beach Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Pompano Beach, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
